Markup and margin are not the same.

Markup is your profit as a share of what the job costs you. Margin is your profit as a share of what the customer pays.

Say a job costs you $1,000 in materials and labor. Mark it up 25% and you charge $1,250. Your profit is $250. That's 25% of your cost, but only 20% of the price. If you meant to make 25% margin, you're short.

To hit a target margin, divide your cost by one minus the margin. For a 25% margin on $1,000: $1,000 ÷ 0.75 = $1,333.33.

One more thing: "cost" should include more than parts and wages. If your price doesn't cover the truck, insurance, and the hours you spend on the phone and the books, the markup is eating your overhead, not making profit.
